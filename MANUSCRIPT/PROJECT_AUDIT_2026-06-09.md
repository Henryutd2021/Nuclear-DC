### 1. Section 45U phaseout applied hourly to LMP instead of the statutory annual gross-receipts test — baseline-year credit should be ~$0, model grants $26.4M/yr, and the headline NOAK viability claim depends on it

- **Dimension:** finance | **Location:** `src/finance.py:49-89 (also src/milp/builder.py:436-450)` | **Verifier confidence:** high

26 U.S.C. 45U(b)(2) computes the credit reduction from gross receipts 'during the taxable year' — one annual test on total receipts vs 2.5 c/kWh x annual kWh. The code instead evaluates the phaseout independently every hour using the hourly day-ahead LMP as receipts. Because ERCOT 2023 Houston prices have a low median ($22.72/MWh) but a high mean ($57.29/MWh, per data/ercot/dam_summary.csv row HB_HOUSTON 2023), the hourly version grants the full $15/MWh in most hours (~$12/MWh average, $26.4M/yr per the manuscript), whereas the statutory annual test at the same prices puts receipts above the $43.75/MWh cutoff and yields exactly $0 for the headline 2023 year. The Methods further assume a PPA where the data center is an unrelated offtaker — but then gross receipts would be the PPA price (which, at the paper's own Case-2 LCOE of $234.5/MWh, is far above $43.75), again zeroing the credit; using merchant LMP as the receipts proxy while assuming PPA sales is internally inconsistent and credit-maximizing. The manuscript states the pre-credit NOAK gap is ~10% and 'the $26.4M/yr Case 1 credit more than covers it' (main.tex:154); with a statutory credit of ~$0 the NOAK margins of +25%/+22% become roughly -9%, flipping the central conclusion that 'at NOAK reactor capital the PTC opens a viable region' (main.tex:92). The hourly credit also distorts dispatch inside the MILP (it pays $15/MWh to generate/export in sub-$25 hours; the paper itself notes 'the production tax credit already drives the reactor to export close to its full surplus', main.tex:172).

**Evidence:** src/finance.py:68-69: 'Here the hourly market price (LMP) stands in for the per-kWh gross receipts.' builder.py:448-449: 'ptc_annual_expr = (-sum(ptc_rate_t[t] * m.P_turb_net[t] for t in m.T) * dt * annual_scale)'. main.tex:354-356: 'Phi_ptc = sum_t c_45U(pi_g(t)) P_tn(t) dt ... using the hourly ERCOT price as the kWh-indexed gross-receipts proxy ... assuming a power-purchase-agreement structure in which the data center is an unrelated offtaker'. data/ercot/dam_summary.csv: 'HB_HOUSTON,8760,57.29...,2023' (mean $57.29/MWh > $43.75 phaseout end) vs median $22.72.

**Suggested fix:** Implement the phaseout as the statute defines it: compute annual generation-weighted receipts per MWh (merchant LMP basis or the assumed PPA price — pick one consistent with the sales structure) and apply one annual credit rate to sold MWh. Report the hourly-LMP variant, if kept, as an explicit favorable bound, and re-derive the NOAK viability claim under the statutory computation.

**Adversarial verification:** The finding survives adversarial verification on every element; all quotes are accurate and the quantitative claims reproduce from in-repo data.

### 3. Willans extraction penalty 0.083 MWe/MWth is physically insupportable for a 7 bar / 165 C extraction (~2-3x too low)

- **Dimension:** physics | **Location:** `config/plant_case2.yaml:54-63 (also src/milp/builder.py:214-221, MANUSCRIPT/Nature Energy/main.tex:254-259)` | **Verifier confidence:** high

The entire cogeneration value calculus rests on the marginal electricity lost per MWth of steam diverted at the HP/LP crossover (7 bar abs, 165 C sat, per data/reactor/bwrx300_technical.yaml:47-49). The config justifies 0.083 via '1 kg/s extraction ~ 2.0 MWth in and ~0.165 MWe lost at the LP stage'. Steam-table check: extraction steam at 7 bar on the real expansion line (h ~ 2428 kJ/kg, x ~ 0.84) has an isentropic drop to the 6.9 kPa condenser of ~578 kJ/kg; at LP isentropic efficiency 0.80-0.85 the lost work is ~460-490 kJ/kg, not 165 kJ/kg. Even with a full hot-condensate-return credit to the feedwater train, the loss coefficient lands at ~0.18-0.24 MWe/MWth, the standard CHP power-loss range for a 7-bar tap. Equivalently, beta=0.083 implies the LP turbine recovers only 29% of the heat's exergy (0.083 / (1-311K/438K)=0.29), vs 75-85% for real machines; 0.083 is the loss coefficient of a ~0.5-1.5 bar (80-110 C) extraction, which could not drive a double-effect chiller. The manuscript claim (main.tex:259) that the coefficient is 'roughly one order of magnitude smaller' than the full efficiency is also wrong on its own numbers (0.345/0.083 = 4.2x). Impact: the SI value decomposition charges only $4.15M/yr Willans cost (main.tex:697); at beta ~ 0.20 this roughly triples, turning the headline '$2.4M/yr, near break-even' absorption result (abstract, main.tex:71,143,198) into a clearly negative ~$8-10M/yr, and shifting the claimed break-even PUE ~1.40 well upward. The promised mitigation does not exist: plant_case2.yaml:56 says 'sensitivity bounds in SI sweep 0.07 to 0.10', but grep of scripts/run_all_analyses.py and the manuscript scenario ledger (G1-G8) shows no Willans sweep at all, and even 0.10 would not reach the physical range. A secondary compounding error: builder.py:225-229 applies the 10% aux fraction multiplicatively after the Willans deduction, shrinking the effective penalty further to 0.9*0.083=0.075.

**Evidence:** plant_case2.yaml:54-56: '# Plan §A7 + §F.1: 1 kg/s extraction ≈ 2.0 MWth in and ~0.165 MWe lost\n# at the LP stage → 0.0825 MWe per MWth.' ; line 63: 'extraction_willans_slope_MWe_per_MWth: 0.083'. builder.py:219-220: 'mdl.P_turb_gross[t] == tb.rated_efficiency * mdl.P_rx[t] - willans * mdl.Q_to_abs[t]'. main.tex:259: 'the Willans coefficient is roughly one order of magnitude smaller than charging the full turbine efficiency'. Steam tables: h_g(7.17 MPa)=2771.8 kJ/kg, s=5.806; at 7 bar h_ext~2428 kJ/kg; isentropic drop 7 bar->6.9 kPa = 578 kJ/kg >> 165 kJ/kg assumed.

**Suggested fix:** Recompute the loss coefficient from a heat-balance model of the BWRX-300 turbine train (or cite a vendor heat balance); expect 0.15-0.25 MWe/MWth for a 7-bar tap with condensate-return credit. Re-run the baseline and the cooling-side conclusions, and add a genuine alpha_w sensitivity sweep spanning at least 0.08-0.25 to the SI.
